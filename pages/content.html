Most of us are good at deciding things and not so good at closing them out. Intentions pile up in notes apps and on the backs of receipts, half-remembered and never finished.

Moonrite is built around one small move: write the thing down properly, on a card that exists just for it. One card a month, on the full moon, and that’s the whole practice.

Each card carries that month’s full moon over an Australian landmark. January is Byron Bay Lighthouse. By December you’ve crossed the country without leaving the kitchen table.

When you’re ready to let it go, you choose the ending. Burning is the signature ritual, it’s optional, and it’s for adults. If you’d rather not light anything, tear the card up instead. Both count.

There’s no streak to keep and no app to check. Miss a month and the next full moon simply comes around again.

You don’t have to believe anything in particular for this to be worth doing. Writing something down and deciding to be done with it is a very old, very human trick.

That’s the whole idea. Twelve cards, twelve full moons, and a bit of room each month to put something down and leave it there.
